### 6.8. UI-4 — Deliberate design brief, identity, and generic-aesthetic review

**Type:** ECOSYSTEM POLICY | **Origin:** USER POLICY | **Applies:** substantial user-facing interfaces before implementation and at design/completion review

Before substantial UI implementation, the App Plan MUST include or reference an explicit design brief and intentional visual/interaction direction rather than leaving identity to a coding model or framework defaults. Address relevant users, purpose, primary workflows, platform, information density, visual personality, tone, accessibility, interaction model, navigation philosophy, references, anti-references, intended emotional impression, and platform conventions. Tiny utilities with trivial interfaces need no extensive design dossier. UI governance applies to either presentation strategy in [UI-1](../3-%20Deciding%20the%20Stack/305-%20Choosing%20Frontend%20Technologies.md#ui-1).

Substantial interfaces MUST demonstrate coherent product-specific decisions about typography, color, spacing/density, geometry/radii/borders, elevation/shadows, iconography/illustrations, motion, layout/imagery/surfaces, navigation, and controls where relevant. The design must explain why this product looks and behaves this way; “the AI generated it” is insufficient. AI-generated work MUST NOT be accepted merely because it functions, looks clean/modern, or responds to viewport changes. **AI convenience must not become visual identity.** Every major dashboard, chart, panel, sidebar, analytics area, or AI interaction MUST have a product purpose; do not manufacture complexity to resemble common generated applications. AI capability requires no permanent chat sidebar, sparkle button, or generic AI aesthetic: contextual, command-based, invoked, workflow-integrated, or external Hermes interaction may fit.

An explicit **AI-Slop / Generic-Aesthetic Review** MUST evaluate the composition as a whole against the brief and product workflows. Review signals include combinations of:

| Signal group | Examples requiring product-specific reasoning when present |
|---|---|
| Color and surfaces | Unexplained purple/blue/cyan or startup-style gradients, gradient text, indiscriminate glassmorphism/backdrop blur, glowing borders, excessive soft shadows. |
| Typography and hierarchy | Unexamined Inter/Roboto/system-sans defaults, oversized centered marketing headlines, interchangeable SaaS heroes, hierarchy dictated by fashion rather than product importance. |
| Layout and geometry | Repetitive three-card layouts, identical rounded-card grids, nested cards, excessive pills/buttons/chips/tags/rounded rectangles or borders, fashionable bento layouts, unnecessary floating cards, domain-independent dashboards, huge empty whitespace reducing useful density. |
| Decorative symbolism | Generic thin-line icons everywhere, decorative blobs, random dot grids, purposeless noise textures, sparkles or generic AI stars/wands. |
| Motion and copy | Arbitrary micro-animation, excessive hover motion or meaningless bouncing/scaling; interchangeable copy such as “Unlock the power of…”, “Transform the way you…”, “Elevate your workflow…”, or “Supercharge your…”. |

These are contextual review signals, not prohibited primitives. Gradients, purple, cards, rounded corners, glass, shadows, familiar fonts, and other techniques remain valid when deliberately chosen, coherent, useful, accessible, and consistent with the design system. Superficially changing a purple gradient to green while leaving an unjustified generic SaaS composition intact MUST NOT count as product-specific design. Review evidence MUST explain relevant decisions and observed workflow/hierarchy suitability rather than merely check off banned-looking features or claim visual uniqueness.

The brief MUST connect relevant differentiation under [DEV-18](../1-%20Deciding%20a%20Project/105-%20Defining%20Goals%20and%20Success%20Criteria.md#dev-18), use context, and content tone to user flows and information priority before decoration: immediate/secondary information, primary/detail/contextual views, progressive disclosure, and competing attention. Complex interfaces SHOULD reason through low-fidelity sketches, text/ASCII wireframes, navigation maps, mockups, or interaction flows first. One-shot AI output is an exploration prototype, not automatic finished design. Develop appropriate drafts, subtract unnecessary effects/containers/labels/chrome, choose concrete art direction, then establish actual flows, systems, states, accessibility, and real-content behavior. Vague “modern”, “premium”, “sleek”, or “less AI” adjectives are insufficient final specifications.

Every generic-aesthetic review MUST ask what can be removed. Also inspect reflexive badge/H1/dual-CTA heroes, exactly three feature cards, identical icon boxes, thin gray borders or colored left edges everywhere, permanent sidebars/floating AI buttons, arbitrary stagger/fade/hover-scale, and dark mode chosen by reflex. Invented metrics, testimonials, and social proof MUST NOT be presented as genuine evidence under [EVID-1](../0-%20Start%20Here%20and%20Universal%20Rules/009-%20Evidence%20and%20Truthful%20Completion.md#evid-1). The logo-swap question SHOULD test whether workflow, information architecture, typography, hierarchy, layout, content, and interaction could belong to many unrelated products; it is a heuristic, not proof or a requirement for novelty that harms usability.

References from software/screenshots/platforms, design systems, print, architecture, photography, physical products, typography, moodboards, or historical UI MAY inform composition, rhythm, hierarchy, interaction, and density. References do not authorize copying proprietary work. Prefer owner-created, properly licensed/open resources, public standards, and original design, preserving relevant provenance. Art-direction dials such as variance, motion intensity, or density MAY clarify the brief; no third-party numerical default becomes universal policy.

### WEB-UX-1 — Living website/app design and UX preferences (2026-11-04 candidate)

**Type:** DEFAULT | **Origin:** USER POLICY | **Applies:** website and application visual/interaction design, whether human-authored, AI-generated, or adapted from licensed sources

This is the **design/UX** category of the owner's living three-category website/app ruleset. The owner's stated items are **defaults, not absolute bans**, unless a separately applicable integrity, security, accessibility, or evidence rule makes a particular behavior mandatory or prohibited. An explicit project-specific request may choose an otherwise avoided visual technique. Assess the composition and actual product needs; avoid inventing additional aesthetic mandates. This refines, but does not replace, [UI-4](#ui-4), [DESIGN-1](504-%20Creating%20Three%20Design%20Directions.md#design-1), [UI-5](506-%20Building%20the%20Design%20System.md#ui-5), and [UI-7](510-%20Reviewing%20and%20Approving%20the%20Design.md#ui-7).

**Avoid by default unless explicitly requested:**

- **Icons and stock visuals:** ubiquitous Lucide or emoji icons; AI stock imagery; sparkles or generic AI-star decoration. Icon choices should not become automatic fingerprints of AI-generated UI.
- **Color and effects:** purple or harsh gradients; generic white backgrounds chosen by reflex rather than intent; neon or pastel defaults; radial orbs; liquid glass. White, purple, gradients, and similar primitives remain usable when intentionally selected for the actual product.
- **Typography, layout, and sections:** oversized hero text; rote use of Space Grotesk; bento layouts and dot grids; generic three-feature-card layouts; terminal mockups; three pricing tiers by rote; repetitive layouts; inconsistent or crowded sections. Do not impose identical designs across unrelated products.
- **Motion and flourishes:** excessive animations; cursor effects; animated arrows; unnecessary motion without a meaningful interaction or feedback purpose.
- **Marketing and claims:** fake reviews, testimonials, metrics, or counters; generic content without real product demonstrations; em dashes in site copy by default. Invented evidence is separately governed by [EVID-1](../0-%20Start%20Here%20and%20Universal%20Rules/009-%20Evidence%20and%20Truthful%20Completion.md#evid-1) and is not permitted merely because a project requests a visual exception.
- **Product usability failures:** poor mobile support; missing real product demos when a product demonstration is relevant. The actual rendered-state and responsive evidence contract remains [UI-7](510-%20Reviewing%20and%20Approving%20the%20Design.md#ui-7).

**Try to employ when relevant to the product and its users:**

- **Visual perception and hierarchy:** aesthetic-usability effect; visual hierarchy; Gestalt proximity, similarity, and common region; Von Restorff effect. These inform legibility, grouping, and meaningful emphasis, not decorative uniformity.
- **Choice, action, and interaction speed:** Hick's law, Fitts's law, Doherty threshold, and feedback loops. Apply to decision complexity, control reachability, responsiveness, and visible system state; do not invent an arbitrary universal number of choices or latency budget.
- **Learnability and information structure:** Jakob's law, recognition over recall, progressive disclosure, Miller's law, and serial-position effect. Familiar interaction patterns and appropriate grouping should reduce cognitive effort without hiding important actions. Miller's law is not a compulsory fixed menu-item count.
- **Workflows and remembered experience:** goal-gradient effect, peak-end rule, Tesler's law, and Occam's razor. Reduce avoidable complexity, prioritize task completion, and make recovery/progress comprehensible without pretending intrinsic complexity can always be removed.
- **Input handling and tolerance:** Postel's law, interpreted only where lenient input acceptance is safe. It MUST NOT override strict authentication, authorization, input/schema validation, data integrity, or secure output encoding.

The principles above are **decision aids**, not a checklist requiring a visual cliché or a claim that every interface must implement every cognitive effect. Distinguish user evidence from heuristics, test important workflows and supported screen sizes, and disclose when a principle conflicts with another goal. Established accessibility criteria and real-world rendering under [UI-3](509-%20Applying%20Accessibility%20and%20Localization.md#ui-3) and UI-7 take precedence over attractive but unusable compositions.

**Maintenance:** Preserve the owner's wording when practical, deduplicate repeated preferences, keep the latest three-category structure, and flag uncertain voice transcriptions as *unverified* rather than silently normalizing them.
