## Finite reconstruction and exactness

The finite-self-reference argument is mathematical reasoning applied to the owner's stated boundary, not a claim that an RFC defines the application's scope. A finite JSON document cannot contain an endlessly nested literal complete copy of itself. A schema can carry finite retained payloads plus a reconstruction specification for generated recovery envelopes. The constitution should require that specification, without choosing its byte layout or saving algorithm.

Separate these categories explicitly:

1. Retained original files, including user-created/imported JSON documents: exact bytes require a reversible representation. Parsing and regenerating equivalent JSON can change whitespace, escaping, member order, number spelling/precision, BOM, encoding or line endings. Raw-byte encoding is one valid approach. Human-readable decoded text may accompany it but cannot silently replace the exact payload.
2. Generated individual/global recovery envelopes: define deterministic reconstruction from payload, schema/version and explicit metadata, including the finite boundary for self-generated identities. Byte identity of a regenerated envelope requires a deterministic serialization contract; semantic equivalence is a separate claim.
3. Retained logical history: every retained historical payload still needs literal recovery coverage under the stated scope. Avoid recursive copies of recovery envelopes and backup containers; do not excuse dropping retained prior user versions as “history.”
4. Temporary transactions, derived indexes and operational containers: classify retention/reconstruction obligations. Excluding recursive machinery does not exclude important user edits, settings, relationships, retained logs or necessary recovery information.

Self-hash cycles deserve explicit treatment: a field cannot generally be assumed to contain the cryptographic digest of the entire finalized byte string including that digest. Require a defined hash domain/envelope rule (e.g. payload domain or excluded-field domain) and deterministic verification; this is an example of a required contract, not selection of its final mechanism.

Metadata exactness also requires a declared platform contract. Original bytes do not recreate ACLs, extended attributes, timestamps, symlinks, permissions or filename semantics automatically. Cross-platform restoration must document unsupported metadata and naming/case/Unicode collisions rather than claiming all filesystem properties are universally reproducible. Restoration paths and symlinks must not escape the selected destination or overwrite unapproved files.

## Storage and scaling calculations

For original binary payload byte lengths `n_i`, let `N=sum(n_i)` and `B=sum(4*ceil(n_i/3))`. Originals plus two single-encoded JSON recovery layers require approximately `N+2B`, before envelopes, metadata, retained history, logs, transaction staging, backup generations and filesystem allocation. For large payloads, this approaches `11N/3` or 3.667 times original size. Thus 100 decimal GB is about 366.7 GB, commonly rounded to the owner's 366 GB example.

This estimate assumes the global JSON contains the original binary payload once in its own encoded representation; base64-encoding an entire already-base64 individual JSON adds another expansion layer. Require the App Plan to show its actual schema's amplification rather than blindly reuse the estimate. Separately encoded chunks each incur padding/framing. Stored compression, reflinks, snapshots and deduplication may reduce physical allocation but require workload/platform evidence; compressed audio may offer little additional compression.

Streaming can bound working memory if parser, serializer and encoder support bounded tokens/chunks. It does not remove total I/O, validation, transfer, or complete-payload obligations. A parser that streams records but allocates each enormous Base64 string may still exhaust memory. Complete generation may require processing the whole global output even for a small edit; hashing alone does not solve this. In-place rewriting, chunk alignment, sparse allocation or copy-on-write optimizations are engineering candidates, not automatically portable guarantees or approved mandates.

Plan measurements for total/peak disk, staging while previous committed state survives, memory including largest token, CPU, bytes written/uploaded, startup/search, interruption recovery, snapshot/history growth, workload frequency and lifetime size. Never silently truncate accepted input or replace literal payload with out-of-file references to meet limits. Surface a material workload conflict to the owner if requirements cannot be met.

## Commit and recovery constraints without selecting Topic 1

Documentation supports distinguishing namespace atomicity, data durability, multi-artifact consistency, concurrent writer coordination, validation and acknowledgement. Require evidence of which revision is committed, pending, failed, disputed or restored. A newer timestamp, successful JSON parse or matching self-supplied hash is insufficient to establish legitimacy. An integrity check protects against a particular expected baseline; it cannot independently prove the expected baseline is correct or authorized.

Preserve the owner's unresolved choices: acknowledgement only after both full copies versus an independently durable pending state; staging and commit format; write coordination; efficient complete global updates; mandated storage optimization; device/NAS/cloud authority topology; batching/rebuild/compaction/capacity thresholds; timeout/retry budgets; history retention. A requirement for truthful state labels must not accidentally choose either acknowledgement model. Until the appropriate mechanism is approved and verified, do not claim its guarantee.

Independent protected recovery points are needed for errors that propagated into both current copies. A backup inside the same deletion authority/failure domain is not automatically independent. Keys, recovery instructions and tools must remain available for a claimed app-disappearance recovery point. No source establishes recovery after destruction of all copies and required keys.
