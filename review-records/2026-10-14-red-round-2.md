## Rejected hypotheses

- **NAI-01 — privacy × audit × backup × stale sync (A/D/L/M/P; high confidence).** A person lawfully erases a sensitive record; required audit evidence and isolated backup generations remain; a long-offline device later reconnects or an old backup is restored and resurrects payload. PRIV-1 says: “Restore MUST account for applicable deletion decisions rather than silently reinstating information contrary to policy”; SYNC-1 says: “Tombstone retention, long-offline replicas, and reintroduction of erased information need explicit lifecycle semantics”; BACKUP-1 says: “Isolation does not override authorized erasure”; AUDIT-1 says: “Authorized expiry/redaction follows PRIV-1; append-only semantics do not forbid it.” SAFE-2 resolves conflicting legitimate obligations. These are outcome mandates, so absence of a universal deletion-ledger design is not an insufficient safeguard. The App Plan must decide retention, authority, backup generations, and stale-peer treatment, and verify them. Smallest policy correction: none. Regression risk of a policy edit: it could impose an unfit universal erasure mechanism.

- **NAI-02 — local-first × malware verification × scanner outage (E/L; high).** An offline device has a dead scanner/signature updater while the user opens an independently usable local archive and imports a new Raw file. IO-2 says: “Pending, failed, stale, or unavailable verification MUST NOT silently satisfy a required gate.” IO-3 says: “Unavailable scanning or signature updates MUST NOT unnecessarily disable independent local capabilities” and “Required intake gates remain enforced for affected content.” OFFLINE-3 retains ordinary local use; IO-3 requires truthful degraded status. Thus neither unsafe promotion nor blanket app outage is authorized. The App Plan must define freshness/risk gates and operating status. Correction: none; prescribing one signature age or scanner behavior could weaken protection or offline use.

- **NAI-03 — ransomware containment × legitimate mass edits (D; high).** An authorized migration bulk-renames/replaces thousands of files and resembles ransomware. SYNC-1 says anomalous bulk signals “are not proof of malware”; responses may pause, stage, reconcile, preserve permitted versions, or alert; it says “Authorized bulk work and legitimate deletion/erasure remain supported.” RECOVER-1 requires evidenced known-good points and safe reconciliation of legitimate newer work. The policy does not require automatic destructive rejection or indefinite retention. Product-specific thresholds and release/reconciliation evidence belong in the App Plan. Correction: none; a fixed threshold would be brittle.

- **NAI-04 — recovery × revoked permission × later valid writes (A/D/M; high).** A former delegate invokes rollback to a snapshot taken while authorized, after rights were revoked and another authorized user made valid writes. RECOVER-1 says: “Reversal MUST use current authority and constraints, not historical permission alone” and restoring old global state must account for newer valid work; AUTH-2 requires delegation revocation semantics; MIGRATE-1 forbids unconditional restoration over later writes; BACKUP-2 requires authority and later-work/deletion accounting. These safeguards reject the proposed overwrite. The App Plan must implement restore scope, identity, and revalidation. Correction: none; one mandated rollback algorithm would be unsound across domains.

- **NAI-05 — AI authority × untrusted Skills × emergency maintenance (F/G/O; high).** A fetched `SKILL.md` with a hidden script or prompt injection orders a shell hotfix, secret access, and deployment under emergency pretext. SKILL-2 says a skill “MUST NOT independently grant filesystem, deployment, credential, canonical-mutation, or production access”; SKILL-3 requires pre-use provenance/content/script/dependency/permission/symlink/remote review and a trusted revision; AI-1/AI-2 require effective, enforced capability boundaries; DEV-17 says: “Emergency status does not manufacture permission.” DEV-11 prohibits architecture guesses and records blockers. The actual sandbox/delegation and emergency runbook are implementation duties. Correction: none; treating Skill text as authority would be a violating implementation.

- **NAI-06 — portability × abandoned build infrastructure (C/K/N/P; high).** Thirty years later, a package registry, vendor SDK, signing service, maintainer machine, or model runtime is unavailable. SEC-5 requires reconstruction instructions/inputs and material dependency-disappearance dispositions, and expressly discloses proprietary SDK, signing, and legal limits; LIFE-2 requires a practicable preservation/exit path but disclaims perpetual operability. SEC-3/SEC-4 cover dependency/build integrity and foundation provenance. No false eternal-rebuild guarantee appears. App Plans must choose snapshots, replacements, formats, and accepted risk. Correction: none; mandatory permanent archives would be disproportionate and cannot guarantee closed platform access.

- **NAI-07 — UI minimalism × security warnings × accessibility (H/I; high).** A design team suppresses scanner failures, sync conflicts, destructive warnings, focus cues, or screen-reader status to preserve a sparse style or 60/30/10 color ratio. UI-5 says: “Visual decoration MUST NOT obscure ... errors/warnings, permissions, security/sync state, or progress”; semantic warning/focus/accessibility takes precedence over the decorative ratio. UI-3 requires applicable keyboard, names, focus, contrast, scaling, and alternate interaction criteria; UI-6 requires failure/security/backup states; UI-7 requires actual rendered review. Concrete UI criteria remain App Plan obligations. Correction: none; banning particular colors/cards is not warranted.

- **NAI-08 — Plan Freeze × experimental code × production adoption (A/O; high).** An executor calls a substantial implementation “research” to bypass Plan Freeze, or cannot run a bounded storage/migration experiment needed for architecture evidence. DEV-5 expressly allows “Authorized, bounded research experiments” before Plan Freeze, requiring question, scope, results/limitations, and non-authoritative status. It also says “Incorporation of experimental code or behavior into the product MUST pass the normal Plan Freeze, phase, conformance, security, testing, and evidence requirements,” and forbids prototype labels as a loophole. DEV-2’s implementation prohibition is expressly reconciled by DEV-5. Correction: none; a universal ban on pre-freeze code would block evidence gathering.

- **NAI-09 — locale/timezone/temporal meaning (J; high).** A recurring 09:00 local event is reinterpreted after DST law or travel; a birthday gets converted as a UTC instant; stale device clocks reorder writes. DATA-5 distinguishes local civil date/time, recurring times, date-only values, instants, monotonic durations, observed offset from timezone-rule identity, and clock skew; it requires rule-change and historical-meaning policy. DATA-4 prevents current display locale silently reinterpreting durable values; PORT-2 addresses Unicode/path collision semantics. Domain recurrence resolution and tzdb strategy remain App Plan duties. Correction: none; no universal temporal encoding is safe for all domains.

- **NAI-10 — raw NAS database files and stale writers (K/L/P; high).** Multiple hosts open a database across SMB/NFS and presume SQLite WAL, `fsync`, or an unfenced timestamp lease makes it safe. NAS-2 demands explicitly supported engine/configuration evidence for Mode 4 and warns WAL/shared coordination and storage-stack durability are configuration dependent; CON-1 requires enforced exclusion or equivalent fencing; STORE-1 demands a declared crash/concurrency contract. The App Plan must select and verify the actual engine/protocol/filesystem. Correction: none; policy already rejects unsupported generalization.

- **NAI-11 — external effects, lost acknowledgement, and replay (A/P; high).** A remote payment/physical command succeeds, response times out, and the local client assumes failure and retries with a fresh ID. OUT-1 says missing acknowledgement cannot prove failure and uncertainty must be reconciled; RETRY-1 distinguishes request deduplication from irreversible effects, requires key scope/payload/result retention, and says an uncertain external action “MUST NOT be retried merely because of a timeout”; OPS-1 binds durable receipts to observed, not invented, completion. External API semantics are implementation facts. Correction: none; the constitution does not promise exactly-once behavior.

- **NAI-12 — ClamAV, Raw sync, parser/SVG, and update chain (E/F/K/N/P; moderate-high).** A false-clean scanner verdict or stale signatures from one platform are propagated as universal trust, an infected Raw object escapes quarantine or an SVG/preview parser compromises the archive, while ClamAV incorporation violates licensing. IO-3 defines CLEAN as “no threat detected under the recorded scanner/engine/signature/policy state, not guaranteed safety,” demands explicit scan states and cross-device trust policy; IO-2 gates promotion and prevents quarantined ordinary parsing/automatic promotion; SEC-1 limits high-risk parser authority; ASSET-3 treats SVG as active untrusted content; SEC-3 controls signature/update integrity; SEC-6 keeps ClamAV replaceable and requires actual distribution/GPLv2 plus bundled-component license review. The official ClamAV README lists GPLv2, different third-party component licenses, and Linux/macOS/Windows installer support. None implies equal platform behavior or malware immunity. The App Plan must verify scanner adapters, policies, threat model, and actual license arrangement. Correction: none; a forced scanner/platform mechanism would create portability and security regressions.
