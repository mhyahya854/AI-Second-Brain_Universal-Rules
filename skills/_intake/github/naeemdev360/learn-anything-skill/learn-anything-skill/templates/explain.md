# Explain it back — {Lesson title}

Close the lesson file. This one is from memory, out loud, in conversation with your tutor.

The point isn't to recite a definition — it's to find out whether the idea is actually
yours yet. If you reach for a term you can't unpack when asked, that's the signal.

Think out loud. Restarting a sentence, changing your mind halfway, saying "no wait" —
none of that costs you anything. I'm scoring the understanding, not the delivery.

1. **Explain {the core concept} to {a specific audience — a new teammate on day one /
   your PM who doesn't code / a rubber duck / in one tweet}.** Your own words; no borrowed
   phrases from the lesson.

2. **Give me an example of your own** — not the one from the lesson, not the one from the
   worked examples. Something from your own work or life.

3. **Why does it work that way?** {or: when would this break?}

4. **Where would you *not* use this?**

You need {threshold}% here, the same as the quiz and the assignment. A confident answer
made of jargon scores lower than a halting one made of your own words.
