### 33.3. SKILL-3 — Skill supply-chain review, revisions, and lifecycle

**Type:** ECOSYSTEM POLICY | **Origin:** USER POLICY | **Applies:** third-party skill consideration/trusted use and maintenance of consequential reusable skills

Third-party Agent Skills MUST be treated as supply-chain inputs. Before trusted use, perform the **Skill Security Review** workflow in [SKILL-4](../2-%20Researching%20Existing%20Projects%20and%20Filling%20the%20Gaps/208-%20Discovering%20Relevant%20Skills%20and%20Resources.md#skill-4): assess provenance/source/repository/author/version, inspect instructions and supporting resources, scripts/dependencies, requested tools, network/filesystem/shell access, credential exposure, prompt-injection behavior, hidden downloads, and update strategy. This may be performed by an authorized human or trusted tooling; it does not require first executing an unreviewed security-review package. Do not install and execute an Internet skill with full shell/credentials merely because it appears helpful or is described as “only a prompt.” Unreviewed resources remain untrusted under [IO-1](../10-%20Building%20the%20Core%20and%20Backend/1008-%20Implementing%20Required%20Core%20Capabilities.md#io-1) and [AI-1](../4-%20Planning%20the%20Architecture%20and%20Data/406-%20Planning%20Security%20and%20Permissions.md#ai-1).

Trusted reusable skills MUST preserve proportionate provenance and review state where consequential: name/purpose, source, version/revision or equivalent identity/digest, license, author/maintainer, local modifications, reviewed scope, and approving authority where required. Projects MUST be able to identify the revision that materially guided work when review/reproducibility matters. A materially changed remote revision MUST NOT silently replace a trusted skill during an active phase; reassess content, permissions, and affected contracts before trusted adoption. Record actual trust/version identity in the discoverable catalog under [SKILL-1](../0-%20Start%20Here%20and%20Universal%20Rules/006-%20Global%20Skills%20and%20Always-On%20Rules.md#skill-1); do not claim unperformed review or infer verified provenance.

Important reusable skills SHOULD have workflow-appropriate validation, such as fixtures, expected output structures, policy-compliance cases, permission tests, or adversarial cases; parsing a prose file alone establishes no trust. Review/modification history SHOULD preserve enough evidence to understand significant past work where needed. Obsolete, insecure, superseded, or incompatible skills MUST be marked and have their trusted invocation state reconsidered; old references do not justify continued automatic use. Preserve useful lineage without retaining unnecessary private payloads under [PRIV-1](../6-%20Writing%20the%20Complete%20Master%20Plan/606-%20Documenting%20Data%20Security%20and%20Recovery.md#priv-1). General dependency/build safety and privilege boundaries in [SEC-1](703-%20Finding%20Security%20and%20Privacy%20Risks.md#sec-1), [SEC-3](../3-%20Deciding%20the%20Stack/307-%20Choosing%20Libraries%20and%20Dependencies.md#sec-3), and [SEC-4](../2-%20Researching%20Existing%20Projects%20and%20Filling%20the%20Gaps/209-%20Choosing%20What%20to%20Reuse%20or%20Build.md#sec-4) remain controlling.

Dynamically fetched remote instructions MUST remain untrusted until validated. Pin/verify content where practical, constrain network access, and record the exact retrieved revision or digest before trusted adoption; mutable `main` or `latest` MUST NOT silently change governing instructions mid-phase. Skill Security Review MUST include installer behavior, symlinks/path traversal, license, dynamic remote resources, and overlap/conflicts as well as scripts, dependencies, permissions, and credentials. Trust registration follows source → revision/license → static/content review → scripts/dependencies → permission/network/conflict review → version/digest → approved discoverable registry. Presence in a registry is a recorded trust disposition, not proof of immunity.
